“Regular cleaning” and “deep cleaning” get used loosely, so it is fair to ask what you are actually paying for. The short version: **a regular cleaning maintains a home; a deep cleaning resets it.** Here is how we draw the line at Chavez House Cleaning in Dallas.

## What a regular (standard) cleaning covers

A regular cleaning focuses on the surfaces you use every day and that get dirty again within days:

- **Kitchen:** counters, sink and faucet, the outside of the refrigerator, the stovetop and oven front, the microwave, and the floor.
- **Bathrooms:** showers and tubs, toilets, sinks, counters, mirrors and floors.
- **Bedrooms and living areas:** dusting furniture, cleaning glass tables, making beds, vacuuming carpets, mopping hard floors and emptying trash.

It is designed to be repeated. On a weekly or biweekly schedule, a standard cleaning keeps a home consistently fresh without anyone having to scrub for hours.

## What a deep cleaning adds

A deep cleaning includes everything above, then slows down on the places that collect dust and grime over months:

- Baseboards, door frames, doors and door knobs
- Blinds, window sills and stair rails
- Light switches, light fixtures and ceiling fans
- Air-conditioning vents and bathroom exhaust fans
- Cabinet fronts in the kitchen, bathrooms and laundry room
- The stove inside and out, and the dishwasher inside and out
- Hard-water stains on shower glass and scrubbed shower walls

None of these look dirty from one day to the next, which is exactly why they get skipped. After a few months, though, they are what makes a home feel “not quite clean” even right after the counters are wiped.

## How to decide which one you need

Ask yourself three questions:

1. **When was the last professional cleaning?** If it has been several months — or never — start with a deep cleaning.
2. **Can you see dust on baseboards, blinds or vents?** If yes, a standard cleaning will not reach it.
3. **Are you starting a recurring schedule?** A deep cleaning first means every regular visit afterward is about upkeep instead of catching up.

If you answered “recently,” “no” and “not right now,” a standard cleaning is probably all you need.

## A common, practical plan

Many households combine the two: one deep cleaning to set a clean baseline, then [recurring standard cleanings](/services/recurring-cleaning/) every week or two, with another deep cleaning once or twice a year — often in spring or before the holidays.

## Add-ons are separate from both

Inside the refrigerator, inside the oven, degreasing cabinets and interior windows take extra time, so they are usually quoted as add-ons regardless of the cleaning type. Mention them when you ask for an estimate.
